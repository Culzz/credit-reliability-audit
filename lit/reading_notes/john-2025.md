## Full-text findings (3 Nov 2026)
- **Dataset is a simulated lending panel, NOT a real public corpus.** The
  paper "draws on a multi-year lending dataset built to capture how real
  credit systems behave", spanning 2015–2024, with *simulated* recessionary
  conditions (employment shock 2020–2021, unemployment 5%→15%+), and
  label drift engineered/observed as default 15%→23%. `race` and `gender`
  exist as features — dimensions LendingClub does not contain — confirming
  the synthetic construction. This materially lowers transferability of
  headline numbers to our real-data setting.
- Feature set: income, credit score, age, employment status, DTI, loan
  amount (+ race/gender/region). Baseline model XGBoost (+ LR, random
  forest), **expanding-window yearly retrain/eval** (2015 baseline year).
- Drift detection: PSI (threshold 0.25 vs 2015), KS tests, JS divergence,
  Chi-square — distinguishes covariate vs label drift. Reported PSI
  income ≈ 0.16, credit_score ≈ 0.017 by 2019.
- Baseline performance: test AUC 0.63–0.66, F1 below 0.07 (fixed 0.5
  threshold); "risk calibration stability degraded" is observed only as
  threshold-balance drift between years. **No Brier/ECE/CITL/slope anywhere
  — no formal probability-calibration audit.**
- Fairness: DPD/EOD/equalized-odds difference per year across race, gender,
  age, income group, region; group "recalibration" adjusts decision
  thresholds per group (fairness mechanism, not probability calibration);
  Method B reduces mean DPD ≈ 0.026 (95% CI −0.035..−0.016, p<0.05).
- Explanation stability: cosine (0.991–0.998), Kendall tau (0.758–0.912),
  Jaccard top-10 (0.818–1.0) between consecutive years — the paper's core.
- Robustness modules: background sensitivity, counterfactual perturbation,
  proxy-variable detection. Statistics: paired bootstrap CIs, t/Wilcoxon.
- Top-3 2024 features by mean |SHAP|: loan_amount 0.055, dti 0.034,
  credit_score 0.023.

## Positioning for our Gate 1 comparison
A *fix* paper (how to make SHAP survive drift), not an *audit* (does
calibration/explanation/decision reliability track AUC?). It motivates but
does not occupy our claim: no joint audit, no decision-flip outcomes, no
label-delay recalibration of probabilities. Risk of overlap: none identified
at abstract level; must check the full text for (i) decision-level analyses
hiding in Section? (ii) calibration metrics beyond fairness recall.
